PERI-SPH framework: stabilization foundation
Date: 2026-10-15 (Asia/Shanghai)

Purpose
A reusable NOSB-PD module on SPHinXsys, with a 3D single-capsule application.
This revision establishes numerical interfaces and reproducible validation.
It does not establish experimental calibration or full-resolution fracture convergence.
The tested build is double-precision, CPU, three-dimensional.

Reusable interfaces
src/shared/peridynamics/pd_numerics.h:
  boundedTimeStep: validate and clip one increment to all remaining intervals.
  PDTimeStep: limit the solid increment by wave speed, speed, and acceleration.
  PrescribedTranslation: exact reference-position motion with consistent Velocity,
    AverageVelocity, Acceleration, and AverageAcceleration.
  ParticleStateCheck: explicit finite-value and positive-scalar checks.
ProjectedPairStabilization in nosbpd_dynamics.h:
  A central, endpoint-averaged penalty for non-affine deformation. The old
  LittleWoodHourGlassControl name is retained as a source-compatible alias.
  Parameters: non-negative coefficient and symmetric endpoint damage cutoff.
  HourglassForce records its contribution in force units.
src/shared/peridynamics/pd_case_config.h:
  Strict key=value inputs; unknown, duplicate, malformed, and non-finite values fail.
  --case-config is removed before upstream command-line parsing.

Core changes
1. Contact friction is computed from wall-relative slip projected onto the wall surface
   tangent plane per neighbor pair and applied once. Initialize wall NormalDirection
   before using friction; prescribed translation preserves those normals.
   Use identical mu and velocity regularization in FromWall and ToWall operators.
   Reaction and applied contact force pass an action/reaction check.
2. Acoustic damping applies an explicit velocity increment. Its diagnostic forces have
   force units and do not overwrite the upstream pressure Force accumulator.
3. Fluid-wall divergence obtains velocity through the material's virtual AverageVelocity
   interface, supporting rigid and deformable walls consistently.
4. Derived stress/shear/pressure fracture criteria dynamically dispatch via virtual/override.
   Symmetric principal-value evaluation uses Eigen's direct self-adjoint solver.
5. J2 solves a nonlinear scalar consistency equation including saturation hardening.
   Backstress = H_kin * internal_strain; the linear resistance is
   G + H_iso/3 + H_kin/2. Viscosity adds eta/(3*dt) to this resistance.
   Positive dt is mandatory for a viscous plastic update. YieldFunc is implemented.
6. Drucker-Prager uses smooth-cone and hydrostatic-apex return branches, including
   saturation hardening. Its internal hardening variable is the plastic multiplier.
   Associated flow, non-negative alpha, and isotropic hardening are supported.
7. Projected stabilization uses K/delta^(d+2), preserving acceleration scaling
   under geometrically similar problems. Endpoint deformation gradients are
   averaged and damage eligibility is symmetric, balancing pair forces.
   This is an independently normalized pair variant, not a claim of numerical
   equivalence to a specific published Littlewood implementation.
8. Non-finite states, singular shape tensors, and det(F) < 0.05 raise errors.
   The determinant threshold is a numerical admissibility guard, not a fracture law.
   NumericalFailureReason is separate from physical Damage and ParticleLive.

Capsule application
Geometry defaults: outer diameter 1 mm; shell thickness 20 um; nominal three layers.
Shell defaults remain generic, without an established material calibration.
The default liquid is rho=935 kg/m3, dynamic viscosity 0.25 Pa*s, artificial sound
speed 25 m/s. Core viscosity is implemented through the upstream viscous-force
operator, including its reaction on the shell. It is not random velocity damping.
The default plate speed is 0.1 m/s and stroke is 0.58 mm.

The fluid and PD solvers consume the same accepted outer increment; the clock is
advanced with that increment. Output and end times land on prescribed boundaries.
An explicit fluid-solid exchange increment is limited to max_pd_substeps times the
current PD stability increment (default six). This is a configurable conservative
control, not a general stability theorem. Validate it for every new problem.
Bond checks use PD-substep count; bond_check_interval is in PD substeps.
The plastic-strain criterion uses maximum principal value of the average plastic
strain tensor, with its existing stretch safeguard; it is not equivalent plastic strain.

The enclosed core defaults to continuity-based density evolution with volume update.
Optional reinitialize_density uses the original free-surface density summation and
requires separate validation. Fracture and outflow need additional free-surface checks.
Dry control still allocates frozen fluid particles because the upstream body requires
particles; with_liquid=false prevents their integration and load contribution.
PD reference neighborhoods must remain fixed, and PD particles must not be sorted.
The capsule now supports full PD disk restart through pd_checkpoint.h and
--pd-resume; see PERI_SPH_CHECKPOINTS.txt. Upstream XML restart stays rejected.

Outputs
output/effective_parameters.cfg: all active controls in SI units.
output/diagnostics.csv: time, prescribed stroke, step counts, liquid mass, maximum
  speed, density deviation, damage, numerical failures, kinetic energies, force parts.
output/UpperPlate_TotalReaction.dat: shell-contact and direct fluid pressure/viscous
  contributions and their sum; no longer only shell repulsion.
output/run_status.txt: completed or failed, phase, time, counters, failure reason.
VTP: density, pressure, mass, shell velocity, stress, deformation gradient, plastic
  variables, bond count, numerical failure reason, and existing force fields.
The status marks failure snapshots as partial steps. Never use them as accepted results.
Density/Mach admissibility limits are configurable and do not replace convergence checks.
Kinetic energies are diagnostics; a full elastic/plastic/damping/work energy budget
and an interface momentum residual are still required.

PERI-SPH execution backend
The SPHinXsys::peri_execution library selects execution for the coupled solver.
Build with PERI_SPH_ENABLE_CUDA=ON to make the FP64 CUDA backend available.
The S-NSNI capsule and local direct-shear drivers accept execution=cpu or
execution=gpu in their configuration files, or --execution=cpu and
--execution=gpu on the command line. CPU is the default.

CUDA currently covers bonded solid interface assembly, bilinear crack
projection, local crack forces and tangent bounds, and assembly of those
crack quantities onto particles. Bonded interfaces and crack contact share
the same sparse particle trace mapping. Crack geometry selection and the
global time-step decision use the host solver. The shared spatial derivative
operator also evaluates the deformation gradients of the live-bond NOSB-PD
shell and the deformation gradients and displacement Hessians of the S-NSNI
core. Its transpose assembles the NOSB-PD stress forces and S-NSNI internal
forces. The existing material updates and PD stabilization use the host
implementation. The case parameters.json records the backend selected for each
phase. The material calibration,
contact law, and checkpoint schema use the same inputs for both backends.

Build and regression
For the existing local Conda environment:
  export PATH=/home/hezhy58/sphenv/bin:$PATH
  cmake --build build --parallel 2 --target extra_3d_PD_framework_tests extra_3d_PD_capsule_compression
  ctest --test-dir build -L peri_fast --output-on-failure
For a different host, configure upstream dependencies as described in INSTALL_HPC.md.

The cantilever and brittle-disc examples also use the shared PD time-step and
exact-boundary controls; both were rebuilt. Their full physical benchmarks were
not rerun in this stabilization pass.

The peri_fast suite includes 17 core checks, four small 3D cases, their output
verification, and an injected-NaN end-to-end failure check.
Small cases: radius 80 um, shell thickness 20 um, stroke 2 um, speed 0.1 m/s,
end time 20 us. They are intentionally thick shells for fast integration regression;
they are not a resolution-reduced substitute for the 1-mm thin-shell experiment.
The fracture control uses a deliberately low stress threshold only to exercise bond breaking.
The full-resolution default capsule CTest is disabled by default. Enable it explicitly
with PERI_ENABLE_LONG_TESTS=ON for long runs; normal executable runs remain available.

Reproducible isolated run
  python3 tools/peri_run.py run \
    --executable build/tests/3d_examples/extra_3d_PD_capsule_compression/bin/extra_3d_PD_capsule_compression \
    --config tests/3d_examples/extra_3d_PD_capsule_compression/configs/full_capsule.cfg \
    --run-dir build/peri_runs/my_unique_run
Use --no-states only when snapshots are unnecessary. --run-root chooses a unique
subdirectory automatically. Existing run directories are refused.
The launcher records executable/source/config hashes, Git revision and changes when
available, a source snapshot, log, return code, and output verification.
A source archive without Git is supported. Rebuild after changing C++ source;
the launcher rejects source files newer than the executable.

Verification covers finite diagnostics, completion, exact time/stroke, positive liquid
mass and its conservation, density/Mach limits, damage range, numerical-failure count,
and reconciliation of reaction components. It is not a quantitative physical-validation verdict.

Evidence from this revision
Exploratory CFL comparisons below were made during stabilization, before the final
wall-surface friction correction. Final regression metrics are stated separately.
The short coupled case with c=25 m/s failed at ~0.8-0.95 us when the fluid-solid
exchange step followed the fluid CFL alone. Disabling the additional acoustic damper
or reducing plate speed alone did not fix it.
Keeping c=25 and material parameters fixed, acoustic CFL 0.06 and 0.03 both completed
20 us. Their maximum recorded density deviations were 0.887% and 0.517%; peak forces
were 9.482 and 9.491 uN. This is short-case evidence about temporal sensitivity only.
The final wall-surface-friction and stabilization revision completed 20 us with
density deviation 0.668%, maximum recorded fluid speed 0.0794 m/s,
and unchanged total particle mass.
The low-threshold dry fracture control developed damage while NumericalFailureReason
remained zero. Injected NaN stopped the run, saved a failure snapshot, and returned 1.

Remaining work before declaring the framework physically validated
1. Original 1-mm, 20-um-shell capsule over the full loading schedule with stable metrics.
2. Time increment, fluid-solid exchange ratio, particle spacing, horizon, shell-layer,
   contact stiffness, and fracture-check interval convergence.
3. Independent elastic beam frequency/deflection and fracture-energy benchmarks.
4. Closed-cavity and moving-wall FSI tests with work/energy and interface momentum budgets.
5. Damage-aware stabilization, objectivity after bond breaking, and post-fracture support.
6. Extend the capsule disk-restart registration and verification to other cases.
7. Align measured size, shell thickness, core composition and loading rate; fit shell
   elastic, yield, viscous, hardening and fracture parameters, then validate independently.
8. Define released mass with a moving boundary or control surface and a mass-flux balance.
Do not interpret disappeared or invalid particles as released liquid. The historical B3
run contains invalid liquid coordinates and is not an accepted calibration result.

Stabilization normalization evidence
The pre-correction geometric-scaling test gave relative error 3: doubling all
lengths doubled the stabilization acceleration instead of halving it.
A non-affine cuboid also had an unbalanced stabilization resultant of 1.639 N.
The revised operator passes inverse-length acceleration scaling, affine nullspace,
and resultant-momentum checks. Its coefficient and damage cutoff remain numerical
controls requiring independent convergence studies and an energy budget.
The penalty-force concept was checked against the primary Peridigm implementation
https://github.com/peridigm/peridigm/blob/master/src/materials/correspondence.cxx
and the control-scheme discussion in Zhang et al. (2023), Materials 16, 4050,
https://doi.org/10.3390/ma16114050. The revised normalization and endpoint pairing
follow the dimensional and momentum checks of this project.
